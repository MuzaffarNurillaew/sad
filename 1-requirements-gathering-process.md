# Requirements Gathering Process

Two methods were planned for this section: a semi-structured interview with the director, and a worker observation plus artifact review of the paper notebook and pDaftar app (full plan in `resources/research/1-requirements-gathering-process-research.md`). Only the director interview has been carried out with recorded evidence to date; the worker observation and artifact review remain planned, not yet conducted.

A semi-structured interview, rather than a fixed questionnaire, fits this business because no written procedures exist anywhere on the farm — the director carries the whole operation in his own working memory, including cost structure, supplier terms, and feed schedules that are adjusted case by case. A rigid survey would miss the conditional logic (e.g. "if this factory's feed, then this many days") that only surfaces when a follow-up question is asked.

The interview was conducted on 2026-10-26, lasted 51 minutes, and opened with an explicit consent request before recording began — translated from Uzbek: "before we talk, I need to ask your permission to record this conversation" (Uzbek: "suhbatdan oldin bu suhbatni yozib olishga ruxsat so'rashim kerak") — to which the director agreed. Audio, video, and a speech-to-text transcript were retained as `evidences/Interview_Director_2026-10-26.*`; the quotes below are drawn directly from that transcript. The raw transcript carries no speaker labels, so quotes are limited to clauses that read unambiguously as the director's own statement, not the interviewer's question.

On procurement, the interview confirmed and extended the contract basis already stated in the business case. On where the chicks come from — director, translated from Uzbek: "mostly Tashkent — chicks come from Tashkent, Guliston, the valley; they come out very good from the valley, so we bring a lot from there" (Uzbek: "koʻpincha toshkent jo'jalarni toshkent guliston vodiy judayam juda ham yaxshi chiqyapti vodiydan judayam olib kelamiz"). On the feed arrangement specifically — director, translated from Uzbek: "roughly, one batch's feed happens on credit, over about 60 days... you pay at the end; a contract is made — we have a formal annual contract, and it stays in effect throughout the year" (Uzbek: "bir partiya nasiya orqali bo‘ladi ya'ni 60 kun... oxirida toʻlaysiz shartnoma qilinadi rasmi yillik shartnomamiz borishni qilinadi va yil davomida amal qiladi"). This is a detail the business case does not yet have: the feed contract is annual and settled on roughly a 60-day credit cycle, not paid upfront per order.

On feed quality, the director linked timing to outcome in a way not previously documented — translated from Uzbek: "the better-quality factory you buy the first feed from, the higher the resulting meat quality ends up" (Uzbek: "qanchalik sifatli zavoddan birinchi olib kelsang uni goʻshtni sifat dalasa shunchalik oshadi").

On pricing, the director gave a concrete recent example of how fast the market moves — translated from Uzbek: "just now, when the price was up I had birds ready and sold — but within 10 days the price had dropped; where I'd sold at 27,500 [UZS/kg], it was down to 23,000" (Uzbek: "mana hozir man bo'ldim ko'tarilganda men suyib bo'ldim buni uje 10 kun oraliqda uje goʻsht tushib ketgan... men 27 yarimga sotgan bo'lsam 23 ming"). This matches the business case's framing of all three buyer prices as market-set, not farm-set, now with a real figure behind it.

On record-keeping, the director's own account goes further than the business case currently does. He wants pDaftar to separate figures by batch rather than pool them — translated from Uzbek: "I want to see everything in one database... I want there to be separate sections inside it [for each house/batch]" (Uzbek: "man hamma narsani bitta bazada koʻrishni xohlayman-da... tovuq xonachalar bo'lishi xohlayman"). Asked about the shape of any future tool, he was explicit that it should be phone-based, not desktop-based — translated from Uzbek: "a phone format suits me better, because I'm on the road most of the time — I don't have one fixed place I sit" (Uzbek: "menga telefon ko‘rinishda boʻlsa qulayroq chunki men ko'proq vaqtim yoʻlda bo'ladi bir joyda muqim turadigan joyim yoʻq").

These are stated here as findings, not as requirements: the interview surfaced that cost and profit data in pDaftar is not separated by batch, that feed-quality timing is a real but undocumented variable, and that any future tool would need to be phone-first rather than desktop-first. Section 7 traces its functional and non-functional requirements back to gaps like these; this section stops short of drafting them.

The one disclosed gap: the brief requires data from at least one "prospective user" who is not the owner, and nothing gathered so far is sourced from a worker, buyer, or other stakeholder — only the director has been interviewed to date. The planned worker observation and artifact review close this gap and should be carried out before this section is considered complete.
