# Business Case

Nurota Halol Parranda MCHJ is a family-owned broiler (meat-chicken) farm based in Nurata, Navoi region, Uzbekistan, run personally by its director, who owns the operation and bears its full financial risk. It raises broiler chickens for meat across two houses, each holding 5,000–10,000 birds per batch.

Procurement and production: day-old chicks come from suppliers in Samarkand or Tashkent, delivered by Damas van, and feed and vitamins are sourced informally through the director's Telegram contacts. The director times each new chick order to when a house is cleaned and ready after the previous batch, adjusted for season and current chicken, feed, and meat prices. Day-to-day, 2–4 permanent workers check each house every 15–30 minutes rather than on a fixed schedule, helped by cameras feeding an Android monitoring app; one worker tallies dead chicks daily, though the count — like the director's other production statistics — is only an approximation.

Slaughter and distribution: a full cycle, chick to empty house, takes about 60 days, but a chicken's own growth period is only 40–50 days — the rest is spent cleaning and preparing the house for the next batch. Slaughtering happens in stages, not all at once: around day 25 the director culls sick or stunted chicks early so feed isn't wasted on birds with no real chance of reaching sale weight; around day 35–40 roughly 4,000 birds are slaughtered to free up space, since the remaining birds then grow faster and die less; and throughout the cycle, small client orders of 50–100 birds are slaughtered on demand by the director and workers themselves, with no external butchers. The main slaughter is done overnight by a hired team of about six — a butcher, a scalding-water handler, a plucker, and two to three organ-cleaners — processing 1,000–1,200 birds per session. Its timing is decided by weighing a random sample of about ten birds: based on their average weight, the director calls the butchers immediately or waits three to five more days, since broiler growth slows with diminishing returns rather than continuing indefinitely. The meat is then sold through three channels — a bazaar agent (its biggest buyer), an individual seller in Nurata, and a small shop at the director's house — at roughly 26,000, 27,000, and 28,000 UZS/kg respectively; none of these buyers are farm employees, and the farm does not set these prices, which shift constantly with the market.

Record-keeping: the director logs feed use by hand in a paper notebook and tracks costs separately in a generic bookkeeping app (pDaftar); camera footage, the notebook, and the app are never linked, and no price history is kept at all, so buying and selling decisions rely entirely on the director's intuition and experience.
