# Business Case

The business is a family-owned broiler (meat-chicken) farm based in Nurata, Navoi region, Uzbekistan. It has no registered trade name; it is run personally by its director, who owns the operation and bears its full financial risk. Its sole activity is raising broiler chickens to slaughter weight and selling the meat, mainly through Navoi bazaar.

The farm has two broiler houses, each holding a batch of 5,000–10,000 birds, and runs a single batch cycle, from day-old chick to sale-ready bird, over roughly 60 days. Day-old chicks are brought in from suppliers in Samarkand or Tashkent, transported to Nurata in one or two small Damas vans. Day-to-day care is handled by 2–4 permanent workers, who check each house roughly every 15–30 minutes rather than on a fixed schedule; four cameras per house feed an Android monitoring app the director also checks remotely. One worker tallies dead chicks each day, though the count is an approximation rather than an exact figure, as are the director's broader production statistics.

Feed and vitamins are sourced through contacts the director maintains over Telegram; there is no fixed supplier contract. When a batch reaches sale weight, the director hires a team of temporary workers for a single overnight slaughter session: one butcher, one worker managing the scalding water, one plucking worker, and two to three workers cleaning internal organs — around six people in total — who process 1,000–1,200 birds in one night. The meat is driven to Navoi bazaar early the same morning in a single delivery run, paid for as a one-off fee each time rather than under a recurring contract. At the bazaar, a worker acting as the farm's sales agent weighs the delivered meat and records the total kilograms in a paper notebook. Prices are not set by the farm: birds and meat sell at whatever the general bazaar rate is on the day.

Record-keeping otherwise is manual and fragmented. The director separately logs feed usage by hand in a paper notebook and tracks costs in a generic bookkeeping app (pDaftar) on his phone. Camera footage, the notebooks, and the app are not linked in any way, so no single record shows the full picture of a batch. The farm also keeps no running history of chicken, feed, or meat prices, even though all three fluctuate frequently — purchasing and selling decisions are made on the director's intuition and experience rather than on tracked data.
