# Business Case

Nurota Halol Parranda MCHJ is a family-owned broiler (meat-chicken) farm in Nurata, Navoi region, Uzbekistan, run across two houses by its owner-director, who bears full financial risk.

Procurement: day-old chicks come from suppliers in Samarkand or Tashkent, delivered by Damas van, in numbers matching each house's 5,000–10,000-bird capacity, under a standing contract paid by bank transfer; a new order for a given house falls roughly every 60 days, once cleaned after the previous batch. Feed and vitamins follow the same contract, timed by season and current prices.

Once chicks arrive, day-to-day production work begins. 2–4 permanent workers check each house every 15–30 minutes rather than on a fixed schedule. When a chick dies, the worker who finds it reports the death verbally to the director, who logs it by hand in the same notebook as feed use; like the director's other production statistics, the resulting count is only an approximation. Cameras separately feed an Android monitoring app.

Slaughter happens in stages, not all at once. A full cycle, chick to empty house, takes about 60 days, but a chicken's own growth period is only 40–50 days — the rest goes to cleaning and prepping for the next batch. Around day 25 the director culls sick or stunted chicks early so feed isn't wasted on them. Around day 35–40 roughly 4,000 birds are slaughtered to free up space, letting the rest grow faster and die less. Throughout the cycle, small client orders of 50–100 birds are slaughtered on demand, in-house.

Main slaughter: its timing is set near the cycle's end by weighing a sample of about ten birds. Based on their average weight, the director calls in the hired crew immediately or waits three to five more days, since growth slows afterward. Once called, the crew of about six works overnight, divided by task. A butcher kills the bird, paid per head at a count agreed verbally. A scalder loosens its feathers with hot water, and a plucker removes them. Finally, two to three workers clean internal organs; together the crew processes 1,000–1,200 birds a session.

Distribution: the meat then goes to three independent buyers, none of them farm employees. The largest volume goes to a bazaar agent at Navoi bazaar, a middleman reselling to seven to nine other sellers, who pays only around 26,000 UZS/kg — a little below the 27,000 UZS/kg a Nurata-based individual seller pays directly. The smallest channel, a shop at the director's house, sells directly to local customers at roughly 28,000 UZS/kg. None of these three prices is set by the farm — all shift with the market. Daily, the agent photographs the kg totals, sends them via Telegram, and settles by cash or transfer.

Record-keeping: the director logs feed use and chick deaths by hand in a paper notebook, and tracks costs, revenue, and profit separately in a generic bookkeeping app (pDaftar); camera footage, notebook, and app stay unlinked, and with no price history kept, decisions rely on the director's intuition and experience.
