# Business Case

Nurota Halol Parranda MCHJ is a family-owned broiler (meat-chicken) farm based in Nurata, Navoi region, Uzbekistan, run by its director, who owns it and bears full financial risk. It raises broiler chickens for meat across two houses.

Procurement: day-old chicks come from suppliers in Samarkand or Tashkent, delivered by Damas van, in numbers matching each house's 5,000–10,000-bird capacity; a new order for a given house falls roughly every 60 days, once cleaned after the previous batch. Feed and vitamins are sourced the same way, through the director's Telegram contacts; timing also follows season and current chicken, feed, and meat prices.

Once chicks arrive, day-to-day production work begins. 2–4 permanent workers check each house every 15–30 minutes rather than on a fixed schedule. One of these workers tallies dead chicks daily, though the count — like the director's other production statistics — is only an approximation. Cameras feed an Android monitoring app.

Slaughter happens in stages, not all at once. A full cycle, chick to empty house, takes about 60 days, but a chicken's own growth period is only 40–50 days — the rest is spent cleaning and preparing the house for the next batch. Around day 25 the director culls sick or stunted chicks early so feed isn't wasted on birds with no real chance of reaching sale weight. Around day 35–40 roughly 4,000 birds are slaughtered to free up space, since the remaining birds then grow faster and die less. Throughout the cycle, small client orders of 50–100 birds are slaughtered on demand by the director and workers alone, with no outside hires.

Main slaughter: its timing is decided separately, near the cycle's end, by weighing a random sample of about ten birds. Based on their average weight, the director calls in the hired crew immediately or waits three to five more days, since broiler growth slows with diminishing returns rather than continuing indefinitely. Once called, the crew of about six works overnight, divided by task. A butcher kills the bird. A scalder loosens its feathers with hot water, and a plucker removes them. Finally, two to three workers clean internal organs; together the crew processes 1,000–1,200 birds a session.

Distribution: once slaughtered, the meat is sold to three independent buyers, none of them farm employees. The largest volume goes to a bazaar agent, an independent trader at Navoi bazaar, who pays roughly 26,000 UZS/kg. A second buyer, an individual seller based in Nurata, pays roughly 27,000 UZS/kg. The smallest channel is a shop at the director's own house, selling directly to local customers at roughly 28,000 UZS/kg. None of these three prices is set by the farm — all shift continuously with the market.

Record-keeping: the director logs feed use by hand in a paper notebook and tracks costs separately in a generic bookkeeping app (pDaftar); camera footage, the notebook, and the app are never linked, and no price history is kept at all, so buying and selling decisions rely entirely on the director's intuition and experience.
