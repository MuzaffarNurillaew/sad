• It didn’t have all the features.

Some failure examples:
• Apple Copland OS: In 90’s, Apple tried to build its own OS. The feature
list developers wanted was constantly growing. They failed to produce it.
They purchased NeXT, which was the base of MacOS today.
• Sainsbury's warehouse automation: Following Tesco’s automation
success, Sainsbury’s tried to automate their warehouse using barcodes to
streamline their operations and increase the efficiency. It was a fiasco.
Their scanners didn’t read the barcodes. After 2 years, Sainsbury’s
scraped the system and declared £150 million IT loss.
• FBI Virtual Case File: The FBI's Virtual Case File (VCF) project was a
major failure, costing $170 million and leading to the termination of the
project without a usable system.

What are the causes of failure?
• Unclear goals and objectives: We don’t know what we are building.
• Scope creep: Ever expanding scope.
• Poor project management: Example, didn’t do feasibility study.
Developers are slow.
• No stakeholder engagement: We build whatever we want, not what you
need.
• Resistance to change: People are afraid they will be replaced and will
sabotage the project.
• Poor planning: Example, inadequate resources (e.g. budget).

System Analyst
A systems analyst studies the problems and needs of an organization to determine how
people, data, processes, communications, and information technology can best accomplish
improvements for the business. When information technology is used, the analyst is
responsible for:
• Efficient capture of data from business source
• Flow of data to the computer
• Processing and storage of data by computer
• Flow of useful and timely information back to the business and its
people
