XP: Values
1. Communication – promote among the team members, also
involve end users actively.
2. Simplicity – design the simplest solution. Code for today, not
tomorrow. "You ain’t gonna need it" (YAGNI) approach.
3. Feedback – from unit tests, acceptance tests, customer.
4. Courage – to change your code or throw it away.
5. Respect – your peers. Don’t commit a change that breaks
tests or compilation. Make sure everyone feels appreciated
and heard.

Lessons learned…
1. Projects fail and fail a lot – Remember Research paper from Tutorial 1.
2. There are many factors that lead to project failure:
1. Not clear, vague or changing requirements
2. Undefined project scope
3. Financial, technical, human resource aspects
4. Developers postpone
5. etc.
3. What should we do? – Manage our projects and the whole process.
4. How can we do it? – Use development methodologies that fit our context and address
problems that we have (i.e. use them for their “side effects”).
Business Information Systems 9

Biggest problems…
1. Not clear, vague or changing requirements is one of the biggest
problems in real life software development.
2. How can we address this problem?
What can we do to reduce the risk of project failure that is caused by (a)
vague or (b) changing requirements?
a) Devote a stage for requirements gathering; use different techniques we covered to
gather quality requirements (involve end-users, prototyping, etc.); document
requirements;
b) Split the project into smaller parts; start with the most important functionalities; write
system that considers possible code changes; etc.

New/Modern methodologies
• So, this is where many of the Incremental and Iterative models, as well as
many of the new/modern methodologies come in.
• By splitting projects into smaller pieces, it is easier to manage and change
them (compared to a big plan ahead).
• All methodologies, that we will see, base their ideas on this simple
premise: divide and concur.
• Every project has a risk of failure. We need to manage risk. Spiral model
puts emphasis on evaluating and addressing risk.

Business Information Systems 15

Spiral Model
• Divide project into smaller ones.
• Iterate through each using the following stages:
1. Identify objectives, alternatives, and constraints;
2. Evaluate alternatives, identify and resolve risks;
3. Develop and verify deliverables;
4. Plan the next iteration.
