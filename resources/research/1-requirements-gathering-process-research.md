## Why 3 methods, not 2

Rubric row (Requirements item 2, 15 points) needs: ≥2 methods clearly stated; methods well justified for this business; data collected effectively including from prospective users (not just the owner); evidence of actually carrying it out (missing evidence = -10 points). Three methods in one visit covers two stakeholder types and gives margin above the minimum.

1. **Semi-structured interview — Owner/Manager (the director).** A small broiler farm has no written SOPs — the director carries the process in his head. Interviews allow follow-up questions and build on an existing relationship, better suited than a rigid questionnaire for an undocumented process.
2. **Direct observation + contextual mini-interview — a Farm Worker.** The owner's description of "how things happen" may be idealized; observation reveals the real day-to-day process. This is also what satisfies the brief's requirement for data from "prospective users" who aren't the owner.
3. **Light document/artifact review — existing farm records.** Ask to see/photograph whatever's already used to track things (notebook, pDaftar app, Telegram messages with suppliers/buyers). If nothing exists beyond what's already known, note that explicitly — informality is itself a finding.

## Question sets

**A. Owner/Manager interview (30–40 min, record with consent)**
1. Walk me through what happens from the day a batch of chicks arrives until that batch is sold — what are the main stages?
2. How do you decide how many chicks to order, and from which supplier, and when?
3. How do you currently keep track of a batch — its age, feed given, vaccinations, deaths?
4. Walk me through a typical morning — what's the first thing you or your workers check?
5. Walk me through what happens when a bird gets sick or dies — who notices first, who decides what to do?
6. How do you decide when a batch is ready to sell, and walk me through how a sale/delivery to a buyer actually happens?
7. How do you currently handle payments and records with suppliers and buyers?
8. What's the thing that currently wastes the most time or causes the most loss?
9. If you could instantly know one piece of information about the farm right now, what would it be?
10. Who else works on the farm day to day, and what does each person actually do?

**B. Worker interview/observation (20–30 min, done while they work)**
1. Walk me through what you do from the moment you arrive at the farm each morning.
2. Walk me through how you decide how much feed/water to give today.
3. If there's more than one batch of birds at once, how do you tell them apart or keep them straight?
4. Walk me through exactly what you do if you spot a sick or dead bird.
5. How do you know when feed, water, or medicine is running low, and what do you do about it?
6. Who do you tell when something goes wrong, and how?
7. Is there anything you keep track of in your head or on paper that nobody else writes down?
8. What's the most annoying or confusing part of how things currently work?

## Evidence capture plan (prevents the -10 point penalty)

Before leaving: confirm a specific time (ideally during feeding/health-check routine so observation + interview happen in one window). Bring: phone for audio recording, this question sheet, a notebook, camera for artifact photos. Get verbal consent before recording and note it (e.g. "Verbal consent given at 09:02").

During the visit, log for each session: date, location, interviewee name & role, method, duration, who took notes. For the interview: timestamped running notes with a few exact quotes. For the observation: a timestamped activity log (e.g. "09:14 — worker scoops feed by eye, no scale used"). Photograph any existing records with permission.

Same day: write a 3–5 bullet summary per session, a transcript excerpt (5–10 exchanges) for the owner interview, the timestamped log for the worker session. Save dated files, e.g. `Interview_Owner_2026-10-26.m4a`, `Observation_Worker_2026-10-26.docx`, `Artifact_FeedNotebook_2026-10-26.jpg`.

Suggested visit timeline (~2 hours): arrival/consent (10 min) → owner interview (40 min) → worker observation (30 min) → photograph records (15 min) → wrap up (10 min).
